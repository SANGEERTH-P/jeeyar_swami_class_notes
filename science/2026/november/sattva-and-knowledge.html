Does sattva lead to knowledge?

November 2026

Our shastras say that sattva leads to knowledge. Yet many people in the West eat meat every day, live largely in rajas and tamas, and still produce remarkable knowledge in science and technology. Is that a contradiction? It is not, because the word “knowledge” is being used in two very different senses.

  1. Two kinds of knowledge
  2. Adhikara comes first
  3. Sattva is the foundation

1. Two kinds of knowledge

The knowledge that builds machines and theories can be gained in any state of mind. When the shastras say that sattva leads to knowledge, they mean something else: the knowledge that discerns “I am the atman, distinct from this body”. That discernment is what sattva makes possible. Worldly brilliance in rajas or tamas does not contradict it, because it is not the knowledge being spoken of.

2. Adhikara comes first

Without first understanding adhikara, one’s fitness for a pursuit, plunging into svanubhuti, direct experience of the self, makes no sense. It is like a boy who has not yet reached adolescence trying to learn about having children: the subject is real, but he is not yet ready for it.

Sattva guna is not a light matter. Svanubhuti is the end, not the means; the means is sadhana. And the very first part of sadhana is cultivating sattva, the part that is most often neglected.

3. Sattva is the foundation

According to our shastras, sattva is the foundation of all genuine enquiry; the natural process of enquiry rests on it. Through sattva the subtle body, the sukshma sharira, is purified, and from that comes chitta shuddhi, purity of mind.

Think of muddy water left to stand. Nothing is forced; the impurities simply settle, and the water above becomes clear. Sattva works on the mind in the same way.

The Bhriguvalli of the Taittiriya Upanishad explains this clearly, beginning Bhrigu’s enquiry with food, and the Gitopanishad (the Bhagavad Gita) lists in detail the foods that belong to each guna.

4. How sattva is cultivated

Three supports work together.

Table 1. The three supports of sattva
SupportWhat it means
Ahara (food)Sattvic food. Some foods are sattva promoters, which do not obstruct sattva; others are sattva providers, which actively give it.
EnvironmentA quiet, natural, oxygen-rich place, preferably one that people seldom frequent, for sadhana.
Sahavasa (company)Anukulya sahavasa: the company of people who support the pursuit rather than pull against it.

5. Who is the adhikari?

The person fit for Upanishadic knowledge is one who:

  • is free from material stress, that is, from the basic bonds of livelihood;
  • is a serious seeker, not merely a collector of information;
  • is in good mental and physical health.

Approaching this knowledge in any other, material way will not give a sensible result.

6. Why experiences mislead

When sattva is neglected, what people take for spiritual experience can be no better than a drug-induced hallucination. Such illusory experience is one of the main reasons the pursuit of true experience so often fails.

It must also be understood that this process takes time. It is not for those who are not serious about it.
